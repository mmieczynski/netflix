# Supporting SDK work after a priority disagreement

[Story selector](../story-bank.md) · [Culture framing](../culture-framing.md)

**Use for:** Supporting a decision you opposed; disagreement over priorities. Episode within the agent-platform project. First draft ready for rehearsal, not rehearsed.

## Answer structure

1. You favored event ingress over refactoring the agent SDK and agent-authoring standard.
2. You expected more trigger sources to increase adoption; this was your rationale, not a measured result.
3. Peers favored SDK work because it would enable more improvements to the flagship agent, which they considered more pressing. You accepted SDK work first and discussed picking up ingress immediately afterward.
4. In hindsight, you agree SDK-first was a good call. Once decided, you committed to the SDK refactor and took ownership of it. You report making sure it succeeded; concrete success criteria are not yet supplied.

## Spoken draft

> On the AI platform, I argued that event ingress should take priority over the agent SDK refactor and agent-authoring standard. I expected that enabling more places to trigger agents would help adoption.
>
> Most of my peers favored doing the SDK work first because it would enable more improvements to our flagship agent, which they considered more pressing. I accepted that order and discussed taking up event ingress immediately afterward. Once the decision was made, I took ownership of the SDK refactor and committed to making the chosen approach succeed.

> In hindsight, I agree it was a good call.

## Frame for the question

The strongest evidence is ownership after disagreement. You did not merely stop objecting: you took responsibility for the selected work. Explain the sequencing discussion as constructive planning, not a condition for your cooperation. You now agree with the choice in hindsight; distinguish that later view from your initial preference.

This is the concrete example for the memo's expectation of supporting execution after disagreement. See [culture framing](../culture-framing.md).

## Keep the claims precise

- Most peers disagreed; that does not establish voting or who formally owned the final decision.
- Peers’ rationale was enabling flagship-agent improvements; no specific improvements or measured impact have been supplied.
- Ingress-next was discussed, not confirmed as an agreed commitment or later delivery.
- Ownership and commitment are clear; specific refactor results remain a rehearsal detail.

## Useful rehearsal probes

What concretely made the refactor successful? These strengthen depth; no further discovery is needed before keeping this as the primary disagreement story.
