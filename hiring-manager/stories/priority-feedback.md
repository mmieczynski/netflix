# Feedback about conflicting priority signals

[Story selector](../story-bank.md) · [Gaps](../gaps.md)

**Use for:** Giving feedback to a more senior colleague; unclear coordination and priorities. First draft with learner-reported outcome; recipient reaction remains optional follow-up. Not rehearsed.

## Facts and answer structure

1. Priorities came from a manager, a more senior peer, a principal and an architect; their opinions sometimes differed, leaving the team unsure which direction to follow.
2. You discussed this with the more senior peer. You generally aim to give feedback proactively so it is not a surprise.
3. You proposed having the relevant people in the existing weekly sync and asking technical leaders to make it explicit whether others were aligned.
4. You believe the authority issue was resolved by ensuring stakeholders were aligned. Exact meeting changes and the peer's response have not been described.

## Spoken draft

> I raised a concern with a more senior peer about conflicting priority signals. Our manager, my peer, a principal and an architect all contributed direction, and sometimes it was unclear which priorities the team should follow. I proposed bringing the relevant people into our existing weekly sync and having technical leaders be explicit about whether their direction was aligned with the others.

> My assessment is that getting the stakeholders aligned resolved the conflicting direction for the team.

## Frame for the question

The useful feedback is about the effect of conflicting direction on the team, not the number or seniority of the people involved. Proactive feedback is valid; it need not be confrontational or unexpected to matter. Do not claim that the conversation was uncomfortable if it was not.

For culture discussion, distinguish consulting stakeholders from requiring consensus. Your reported outcome is that alignment resolved the problem. This does not establish a permanent decision-owner model or how a future unresolved disagreement would be handled. See [culture framing](../culture-framing.md).

## Optional rehearsal detail

How did your peer respond, and what observable change showed that conflicting direction had been resolved? Keep the outcome as your assessment; do not invent formal authority changes or meeting attendance.
